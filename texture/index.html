spwashi’s hand

On a sheet of ruled boxes, spwashi lettered the alphabet, Aa to Zz, along a snaking path, then laid it on a painted board and scanned it. Here it is traced into type: two faces of one hand. Where the sheet had no letter for something (a number, a mark, a sigil), a pen measured from the hand draws it, and says so.

          The post the Scriptorium reads. A reader writes a card there and keeps a copy; the scribes read it, and what they do not keep becomes a count with no words in it. Played through lore.land, an address reads the book’s own client file: its subjects, the questions already answered, and a door to write in each.

            Nothing lives here yet

            The address reads (above), but nothing answers it yet. An address is a place to put something; try one that is lived in.

            Letter blocks

            Write a word in the hand. Each letter sits in its own box, as on the sheet; the sounds the word is made of are spelled out under it. What you write and how you tune it go into the address (! the words, = the tuning), so you can pass it along. The scheme is left open, {, so the letters stay in their blocks; close it, }, and they run free.

            The alphabet, and where each letter came from

            Lettered: traced from a box on the sheet (its row and place on the snaking path). Penned: drawn by the pen measured from the hand, until a lettered one comes in from a scan. Tap a letter to give it its own address (*).

              The curriculum

              Everything here is meant to be made, read and questioned by two people of different ages, together. Each step is something to make and a question to ask about how pictures and words are made — from looking closely to making your own.

                Found in the folios

                The folios' held motifs, read across the archive: textures (what a surface is made of), figures (what is drawn) and sayings (what is lettered). Not used yet: each is added here by hand, one at a time, and says which piece it came from. The pieces live on spwashi.com. Each has an address of its own (~).
