What the book was grown from

Before there were chapters there were studies: two of every part of a story, painted to find out what each part looks like. An action, a concept, a ground, a scene, a wonder. The book gave every part one of its twelve sigils, and every study wears it, with a seal of what it is made of. Some of them grew into chapters, and each of those chapters still keeps its study as its motif.

A study you have not reached yet is veiled in the colours of the chapter it belongs to. Open that chapter and you glimpse it, through a window shaped like its kind of room. Keep it, or finish its chapter, and it is yours whole. Any study will show you what in the book is like it: rooms, wonders, people and palettes, alike in what they are made of.

    How to read a seal
    Petals
    one for each chapter, up to the one where the book first has it; a ring of dots is a study not yet planted in a chapter.
    The heart
    its sigil, and its petals start at that sigil's own hour.
    The braces
    the kind of place: [ ] a window, { } a room, ( ) a gathering, < > a passage. The petal tips take the same shape.
    The ring
    what it is made of: Bonk glass, flour, boonberries, frost, ink, brass, warmth or oak.
    The colours
    its chapter's palette, named for a place.
    The berry
    how it leans: a boonberry for the fairy tale, a baneberry for the absurd and the real, a boneberry for the low magic underneath.
    The pips
    who keeps it, each in their own colour.
