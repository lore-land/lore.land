An hour before dusk on the Turning of the Year, everything unfinished in Book Two landed under one curved brick ceiling: the great bakehouse vault whose back flue warmed the bonk-kiln next door. Inside, oak embers hummed red-gold and rising yeast filled the air with a bright, prickling fizz of bread-air.

Every table carried an arrival from the winter’s roads: teal boonberries thawed by torch-ants from the dawnward furrows, savoury smoked boneberries from Bone.land, dry flour hauled down from the belfry loft, and one small stone jar of Mr. BaneWAP’s bitter orchard jam set neatly beside the sugar bowl with a tin spoon standing in it.

Steam curled up into the brickwork faster than the high iron vents could carry it away, dusting the rafters with warm hearth-drift that smelled like summer remembered through a thick coat.

Outside the wide arched doorway, forty hungry clowns stood in a remarkably orderly queue on the winter flagstones, hats tucked under their arms, sniffing the berry steam without jostling a single elbow. Not one of them crossed the sill uninvited; even a clown knows you open nothing before it has filled.

Inside along the warm brick wall, six off-duty mimes leaned comfortably with crossed ankles against completely invisible warming racks, warming their white-gloved palms in front of imaginary dampers while real bakers hurried past carrying genuine loaves.

In the middle of the commotion stood Mr. BoonWAP, directing three oven doors at once like a choirmaster while the washer-up from the scullery sat on an upturned flour tub drinking hot cider nobody had asked her to pour. Along the sunlit window ledge, early bees woken by the kiln warmth sat humming patiently against the glass.

Boof paced a slow circle around the center brick pier, reading the room’s temperature through the pads of her paws. Every neighbour from the last eleven chambers seemed to have wandered in for a moment’s warmth: the porter with dry sleeves, Ida Bell and Noor from the night school, the lockkeeper smelling of canal oak, even the oldest glazier carrying one unpriced windowpane for a serving tray.

The ambient charge inside the domed vault rose beat by beat — yeast pushing against lids, oak embers ticking louder, berry juice bubbling over crust edges in amber beads. Every wall hummed like a kettle two seconds before its whistle.

Beside the central oven door stood the figure in plain lines, watching the crust turn gold. Above his circle head, the black bowler hat still floated at its exact quiet distance, refusing to settle even here. Whatever waited for the Turning of the Year had not yet landed.

1 min

Two heartbeats before the great central pie could catch along its rim, Boof stepped calmly forward and nudged the brass vent lever with her nose. The high damper swung open with a clean iron clack, sending a bright ribbon of sweet berry steam curling straight out over the plaza roofs.

Out in the twilight plaza, somebody tapped a brass fork three times against a water glass. The waiting clowns beamed, the mimes stepped off their invisible racks, and the bakers hoisted the long peels onto their shoulders. The feast was ready to walk outside.

The crowded brick bakehouse vault on Feast Eve: twin oak-fired hearths glowing red-gold, billowing berry steam, rising yeast tubs, stacked peels, and an open archway onto the twilight plaza.

Light Deep ember orange reflected off vaulted brick, catching warm hearth-drift and golden crust steam.

People

  • the head baker wants forty pies out of the brick mouths without a single scorched rim — flour to the elbows, counts oven seconds in his head
  • the polite clown captain wants every clown kept strictly outside the threshold until the first fork chimes — whispers urgently while hiding three spare napkins
  • the washer-up (off duty) wants to finish her mug of hot cider before anyone remembers where the towels live — relaxed, smiling, refuses to rise from the flour tub

Hooks

  1. The shared flue between the bakehouse and the bonk-kiln has trapped too much ember pressure; release the high damper before the sugar glass melts.
  2. Someone misplaced Mr. BaneWAP's small jar of bitter jam; find it before the bakers glaze the main pie too sweet to hold its crust.
  3. Early bees woken by the kiln warmth have settled on the oven latch; persuade them onto a saucer of warm syrup without losing a minute.
