At the Turning of the Year, Bonk City ran its Great Table out past the plaza, straight through the propped-open parcel gate, and down the cobbled slope until its far trestles vanished into the cold river mist. Overhead swung three hundred dent-lanterns, and from the upper windows clerks tossed cheerful handfuls of confetti cut from voided Thursday slips — bright specks drifting like dry snow.

Every voice from Books One and Two found a bench without needing a brass marker. The porter sat next to the carpenter who had measured the lintel; the night school’s adult pupils filled a whole section with Clem Furrow’s laugh and Noor’s cider cups; Mx. BoneWAP tapped the longest trestle leg once with a felt mallet and declared the joinery sound.

At the head of the linen, sitting comfortably with dry hands and a clean napkin in her lap, sat the washer-up from behind the curtain. Beside her stood Mr. BoonWAP — beaming wider than anyone had ever seen him — carrying a stack of warm stoneware plates out from the bakehouse himself.

Halfway down the benches, Mr. BaneWAP passed a little stone pot of sharp orchard jam to a delighted clown, who spread a careful pinch across a warm wedge and declared it the finest supper of the decade. Across from them sat the Genie off-season, balancing a teacup on one palm and refusing to price a single laugh within earshot.

Boof sat calmly beside the trestle leg with a marrow bone set honestly beside her paws — no ribbon, no chosen title, no speeches asked of her. From fourteen inches up she could see every boot settled comfortably under the cloth, and one pair of crisp black ink shoes standing just past the end of the table.

The figure in plain lines stood ninety degrees to the entire seating plan, facing out toward the open lane rather than down the benches. One circle, one rectangle, four lines, two dots — always ink, never named. Above him, the black bowler hat hovered steady in the lantern breeze, neither landing nor leaving.

Then the great ceremonial pie came down the line — the uneven slice cut slightly wider than its neighbours by the night school’s brass dividers, steaming teal and amber on a flat stoneware dish. The carrier was neither a hero nor a herald: merely a laughing feast clerk stepping backward out of a clown’s enthusiastic bow.

His left boot caught a single curled scrap of voided Thursday confetti on the damp stones. The stoneware saucer tilted two degrees past saving, and exactly one warm slice slid cleanly off the rim and landed crust-up on the cobblestones right between the table leg and the figure’s ink shoes.

For three heartbeats the Great Table went quiet. Up in the teal dusk, nine jazz-geese banked overhead in uneven swinging time, honking all nine notes cleanly across the plaza. Down between the granite setts, a polite column of torch-ants hurried out with tiny lifted embers to warm the fallen berries so nothing went to waste.

Nobody could say afterward whether the spill had been prophecy, clumsy boots, arithmetic, or simple manners toward the street — and nobody tried to force the question into a yes-or-no tray. A circle closed too tight can only repeat itself; an uneven slice lets the ring turn onward without resetting.

Fork brass rang again along the linen, laughter rose warm into the river mist, and the figure lifted his shorter right arm once — a brief, quiet turn of one line — before stepping smoothly along the open lane where the lanterns met the meadow. Above him, the hat followed at its own exact distance.

Late that night, Boof trotted past the Round Archive, where the shell-shaped door stood cracked open on its quiet open brace — leave room for the path, the archive’s oldest law said. Out beyond the wall, the dawnward field lay quiet under clear starlight. Under the roots where the story began, the warmth that remembered the Egg sat calm, whole, and utterly unexplained, waiting for nobody to finish it for the land.

Bonk City's Great Table at the Turning of the Year: endless linen trestles stretching from the lantern-lit plaza through the open parcel gate down to the misty riverbank.

Light Warm golden lantern chains swaying through deep winter teal dusk, catching falling confetti cut from voided calendar slips.

People

  • the washer-up (guest of honour) wants a second helping of warm pie and nobody sliding a dirty plate within ten yards of her elbow — easy laugh, clinks her brass fork against every toast
  • Mr. BoonWAP wants to carry every tray cleanly and learn how heavy wet stoneware actually is — cheerful, slightly breathless, genuinely proud of his apron
  • the Genie (off-season) wants one cup of hot cider with zero coincidences attached — dry, amused, refuses to bring out his pan scale
  • the unnamed figure wants nothing announced; stands ninety degrees to the cloth and lets the uneven slice fall where it will — silent; plain black ink against lantern-lit twilight

Hooks

  1. The feast confetti contains one un-voided Thursday slip still valid until midnight; decide who gets an extra quiet day before the Turning strikes.
  2. Nine jazz-geese have landed on the far end of the trestle in the river mist and will not rise until somebody shares the uneven ninth sliver.
  3. Trace the warm glow beneath the dawnward furrow after the lamps go down — knowing beforehand that the Egg will answer no question directly.

Worth carrying out
