1 min

At six feet up, Bonk City is mostly gables, speeches, and people holding their hats in the breeze. At fourteen inches up — which is where Boof keeps her nose and where the actual work of the town lands — the paving slabs tell a much plainer story.

Every guild boot in the morning lane smelled of its own trade: pine tar from the dock porters, tallow blacking from the clerks, warm flour from the bakers, linseed oil from the glaziers. Iron drain grates breathed up white kitchen steam that tickled the underside of Boof’s chin as she trotted through.

Rain puddles laid the whole sky flat between the stones, upside down and bright as mirrors. From the eaves overhead, chime-gutters dripped a lazy three-note tune into the runoff whenever a cart shook the kerb.

scent

1 min

Boof did not chase one scent when three were running together. Along the wet kerb she read them side by side without tangling a thread: hot berry crust pulling toward the bakehouse, cold wet iron still dripping off the lockkeeper’s cuffs, and the crisp, dry, unshaded-paper scent of the figure’s ink steps.

Between two granite setts, a column of torch-ants had built a temporary winter detour round a porter’s resting boot. Each ant carried a tiny ember no wider than a pinhead and kept the lane moving without a single whistle. Build where the land already leans, the ants’ streets said plainly — and step around the heel that has not moved yet.

Every twenty yards a dropped boonberry crumb sat beside a wheel rut, bruised blue-gold against the grey stone. Boof left them where they lay. A dog who works for the land reads crumbs the way an archivist reads signposts; you do not eat the sign while you are still following the road.

Above the linen, everyone faces the podium. Beneath it, shoes point where their owners actually mean to go.

feast

1 min

In the guild yard, three tall trestle tables had been set up for the feast committee. From above the cloth came dignified voices negotiating lantern spacing, seating order, and who ought to carry the first platter. From fourteen inches off the paving, Boof saw the real meeting.

Every polished boot under the table was tapping a different impatience. One steward had tucked a crumpled delivery tally behind his left heel. Another had flour thick on his toes from visiting the bakehouse without telling the committee. A third stood directly on a found line chalked neatly across the flagstones pointing straight toward the cellar chute.

Nobody above the linen looked down long enough to notice Boof pass, or to notice two plain ink feet step neatly round the bench leg and follow the warm cellar draft out of the yard.

scent

1 min

Boof paused beside a warm iron grate where cinnamon vapour drifted up from the kitchens below. A puddle near her left paw held a clear blue triangle of winter sky and the tiny rim of a floating bowler hat turning the far corner toward the cellar alley.

She never guessed at intentions when boot polish, crust crumbs, and warm grates already gave the whole map. Ears level, paws sure on the wet granite, Boof followed the cinnamon draft straight toward the guild basements.

At the tablePlay in this chapter

Bonk City's cobblestone guild lanes seen strictly from fourteen inches up: wet granite setts, warm kitchen grates, boot heels, barrow rims, ant detours, and sky-mirror puddles.

Light Low raking sun across glistening stones, reflected upside down in puddles and softened by white steam rising through iron grates.

People

  • a dockside porter (boots only) wants a dry wheel-rut for his barrow before the kerb narrows — pine-tar boots, whistles in flat thirds overhead
  • the feast committee steward wants nobody noticing the flour on his boots or the tally tucked by his heel — booming above the cloth, fidgeting nervously underneath it
  • a torch-ant route-keeper wants three inches of undisturbed mortar seam beside the grate — clicks antennae once to turn the entire column left

Hooks

  1. A committee steward dropped an unsigned feast tally under the table and kicked it into a warm grate; trace it down the steam trail before the ovens over-bake.
  2. Three distinct scents cross at the corner puddle — berry crust, canal iron, and fresh ink; choose which trail reaches the guild basement first.
  3. A torch-ant detour has revealed a forgotten chalk mark under the mortar pointing to an unmarked cellar hatch.

Roll a d6

  1. A warm iron grate puffs cinnamon steam, briefly erasing every sight above knee height.
  2. A rainwater puddle shows a silhouette crossing a high roof three seconds before its shadow hits the cobbles.
  3. An iron barrow wheel trundles past, kicking a single bruised boonberry neatly across the kerb.
  4. Chime-gutters above the alley ring a wet chord as a door slams two streets over.
  5. Torch-ants redirect their single-file column cleanly around a dropped copper coin without touching it.
  6. Two crisp ink shoes step past at a right angle, smelling faintly of clean paper and leaving no mud on the granite.

Worth carrying out

  • Torch-ants — Watch where they bend their winter street; their detours always mark the warmest seam and the safest footing.
  • A found line — Follow a chalk line nobody remembers drawing; it runs straight beneath the tables people argue across.
