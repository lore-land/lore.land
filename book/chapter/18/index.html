After three sacks spoiled at the parcel gate, Bonk City’s porters moved the remaining feast flour to the driest room in town: the timber loft directly under the belfry louvres. Ninety narrow treads wound up the inside of the tower, every step powdered soft as fresh chalk.

Small brass load-bells hung from the rafters on waxed twine — Bone.land joinery borrowed for the tower so the beams could ring softly if anyone stacked one sack too many. Above them hung the city’s great hour bell, cast thick enough to rattle teacups across three wards.

Chimney-swifts threaded the slatted louvres in quick dark arcs, carrying fragments of square-chatter higher than anyone meant them to go. Boof climbed patiently behind the porter’s heels, shaking white dust off her whiskers every tenth tread. Ahead of both of them, the plain-line figure climbed the center of the stair without smudging his ink.

At the top landing, a single floorboard had worked its oak peg half an inch proud. Nobody had noticed it while stacking sacks. When a hurried apprentice bell-ringer stepped down hard on its lip to check the strike-cord, the far end kicked upward like a seesaw and tipped an open bin of pastry flour clean into the draft.

The action was crisp and completely irreversible. In two breaths the belfry ceased to be a room with air in it and became a slow white lantern. Every shaft of sun through the louvres turned solid enough to lean a coat on. Rehearsal moths, woken early from the beams, spun drunken loops through the haze with tomorrow’s weather blurred across their wings.

The apprentice froze with one hand over his mouth. “Don’t touch the strike-cord,” the porter called from the stair, coughing softly. “Listen before you mend — let it settle first.”

Nobody touched the real strike-cord. Across the loft, however, a solitary mime who had wandered up from the square to escape a troupe rehearsal had already gripped an invisible rope with both white-gloved palms and leaned his whole weight backward at the exact second the clockwork tripped.

The great bronze bell swung. Inside the thick white cloud, the heavy clapper met the lip — and instead of a brass roar that shook every roof in Bonk City, it landed with a soft, velvety muffled thump. A ring of pure flour puffed straight out of all four louvres at once, hanging above the square like a pale halo.

In the center of the white cloud, every person, sack, and beam wore a coat of pale meal — everyone except the figure in plain lines. Not a grain clung to him. He stood sharp black ink against the suspended whiteness, looking calmly up at the silent bell.

Down in the plaza, three hundred conversations stopped at once. People in Bonk City can talk through any brass chime without missing a comma, yet a single hour that fails to ring stops every boot on the cobbles. Heads tilted upward toward the pale halo drifting off the louvres.

Half the square pulled out pocket watches and compared them while the white cloud drifted over the roofs; for the first time all winter, every watch in town agreed to the minute. Up in the loft, Boof sneezed twice, tapped the loose floorboard back into its mortise with one steady paw, and led the way down the dusty stairs.

At the tablePlay in this chapter

The timber belfry loft high above Bonk City square: ninety oak treads dusted in flour, slatted louvres letting in low sun, brass load-bells on twine, and a massive bronze hour bell.

Light Solid golden shafts cutting across drifting white flour haze; crisp ink shadows where light refuses to scatter.

People

  • the apprentice bell-ringer wants the loft swept and the loose plank nailed before the feast warden inspects the sacks — flour-dusted eyebrows, speaks in hurried whispers
  • the loft mime wants to rehearse pulling a rope that makes no noise at all — silent; gestures with astonishing precision toward rafters nobody else sees
  • the tower porter wants the remaining dry sacks kept dry and off the tie-beams — steady, unhurried, listens to timber before lifting

Hooks

  1. The muffled four-o'clock puff drifted over the watchmakers' street and stopped seven pendulum clocks; trace which watch kept moving and why.
  2. One of the brass load-bells has started ringing softly even though no new sacks were brought up the stair; find what invisible weight is leaning on the tie-beam.
  3. Rehearsal moths dusted in flour have scattered illegible weather forecasts over the market; gather three clean wings before the baker sets tomorrow's ovens.

Roll a d6

  1. A chimney-swift dives through the louvres carrying half a sentence overheard three alleys away.
  2. A loose floorboard creaks; stepping lightly turns it into a hidden cache of old rope-tags.
  3. The loft mime pulls an invisible cord and a very real draft slams the lower hatch.
  4. Flour drifts through the sunbeams so thick that lanterns appear two yards closer than they are.
  5. A load-bell chimes a single clear note when someone tells a boast too heavy for the beam.
  6. Through the louvres, the whole square stops to watch a white ring float lazily across the rooftops.

Worth carrying out

  • Load-bells — Hang one from any beam or bargain; it rings clearly the instant someone asks a structure to carry more than it agreed to.
  • Chimney-swifts — Whisper a short message near a warm flue at dusk; the swifts will drop it neatly one ward farther than your voice can reach.
