Chapter One: Dawn in Boon.land

In which a dog finds an egg the maps will not admit.

A wish is not a thing. It is a direction the world has not agreed to turn yet.

The Warm Furrow

unspent wish

1 min
Step into the scene

vantage dog-height at the bottom of a dug furrow, nose a breath from the shell

light first grey dawn over a frost-white field; the only warm light leaks up out of the hole

scent frost, wet fur, thawed loam—and under it snuffed candle where there should be none

  • a single furrow steams in a field otherwise skinned with ice
  • the egg lies in a cup of melt, lamp-dark, humming like a held breath
  • boonberries along the row lie banked and cold, remembering something
  • no nest, no track, no mother-scent—only the heat of a thing not yet happened

Dawn smelled of frost and wet fur. Boof put her nose to the ground and read the field the way she read it every morning.

Everything was where it belonged. The latch lifted at the kitchen. The bucket knocked the well. A blackbird tried the same few notes until one pleased it. Beneath the berry rows, the ants kept their little hearths, and their patient magic warmed the roots through winter; above them, the boonberries slept under ice, each holding a drop of light. Boof followed the rows past the fence that leaned into last year's weeds, past the place where the miller always stopped to scrape his boots. She sniffed a root here and there, and the field gave back its usual answers.

Boof knew this field. She worked for the land, wherever the fences stood, and took her wages in blueberries. Coin was difficult to chew.

She sniffed. Stepped. Stopped. One furrow was warm. Too warm. Beneath it, the ants had stopped working.

Boof dug. First frost, then soft earth, then a little cloud of steam. At the bottom lay an egg the size of a curled cat, dark as an old lamp.

She touched it with one paw. It hummed.

No nest. No tracks. No mother-scent. She went around the hole once more, slowly. Whatever had brought it here had left no way to follow.

Inside the shell, an unspent wish waited. Boof heard only the hum.

Boof
1 min

Ant-warmth smelled earthy and even, like bread left under a cloth. This was sharper. A candle just blown out. Boof knew the paths to the kitchens, the mill, and the grove nobody liked to name after dark. She had never found an egg on any of them. Certainly not one that warmed a whole furrow.

She rested her chin on the rim of the hole. The egg hummed against the earth. Under her chin, she felt it answer.

She would have preferred a trail. Trails usually ended with something you could eat.

When a Berry Remembers

berry memory

1 min
Step into the scene

vantage nose-level along the berry row, the egg humming just behind

light one thin gold seam in a single berry, brighter than the grey dawn; frost sliding off the row

scent cold juice, sweet skin, snuffed candle, and the ozone of something being remembered

  • the awakened berry sits third along the row, its frost freshly melted
  • its light leans past the egg, downhill toward a dark treeline
  • berry by berry the row turns the same way, ice sliding off like sleep
  • the constellation of light points off the edge of any route Boof knows

Every boonberry had been a blueberry once. A wish had passed over it on the way to being granted, leaving a little of its light behind. Most slept through everything: snow, crows, harvest. The town used them for supper and lanterns. The ants kept the roots warm.

The berry nearest the egg shed its frost. A gold seam opened in its skin. Its light leaned toward the shell, then past it, down the slope.

The next berry turned. Then the next. Row by row, a low glow led toward the grove. Boof followed with her nose. The berries remembered this hum. Long ago, it had passed them going the other way.

She looked back at the hole. Something had come home. Why had nobody come to fetch it?

The Maps That Look Away

ledgergenie

1 min
Step into the scene

vantage front paws up on a reading table, the egg between open books that will not hold its name

light high dusty window-light; the egg the warmest thing in a cool grey room

scent old paper, cold stone, beeswax—and snuffed candle that does not belong in a library

  • the egg sits on an open route book that shows no road to where it was found
  • ink beads and runs off the page wherever the archivist tries to write it
  • the field map ends at the berry rows as if the ground stopped there
  • every shelf in the house leans its attention politely away from what has not happened yet

Before the frost was off the fences, Boof had carried the egg to the map-house in an old feed-sack. It was warm as a loaf and considerably heavier. She set it on the reading table. The archivist fetched a book.

Along a fold in the map, two roads touched. Boof knew those roads. A day's walk lay between them. Outside, a cart bell rang beneath the window.

The archivist smoothed the fold. The bell dwindled to a far, thin chime.

Boof lifted her head. The archivist put a stone on the crease. Neither looked away first.

Then he tried to enter the egg in the book. Egg, one. Dawnward field. Unclaimed. The ink gathered into beads and rolled off the page.

He tried a second pen. Boof waited.

Maps keep what has happened. An unspent wish has not happened yet, and the page had nowhere to put it.

The archivist wiped the table. Boof watched the stone. That, at least, he had known how to hold down.

Following the Hum

Step into the scene

vantage low behind Boof as she leaves the map-house steps, the treeline dark at the bottom of the slope

light clear cold morning; the egg glowing gold against her chest, the grove in shadow ahead

scent warm shell, cold air, and the green breath of the grove rising uphill to meet her

  • the egg rides in a feed-sack sling against Boof's chest
  • a hairline of gold has opened along the shell's crown, shaped like a door left ajar
  • the berry rows behind her still lean downhill, pointing the way a granting once went
  • the treeline at the bottom of the slope is already whispering

Outside, the berries still leaned toward the grove. Boof had spent the morning asking a book. The field had been pointing all along.

She settled the feed-sack against her chest, where a pup might ride. A fine gold crack had opened at the egg's crown. It looked like a door left slightly ajar. The hum kept its steady measure. Her paws found another.

Down the slope. Past the last row. At the edge of the field she paused. Behind her stood a house full of maps. Ahead lay the way the berries remembered.

She went ahead.

Boof
1 min

Evening laid long light across the field. Boof was hungry. The egg was warm. For a while, that was all. Behind her, the berries glowed under their leaves. Ahead, the first trees crowded close.

Then a sound came out of the grove. HONK.

Long, bent, and rather pleased with itself. Like a goose that had learned jazz. Boof stopped with one paw raised. The egg's hum slipped into time with it.

She waited for a second note. Leaves stirred. Nothing more.

She put her paw down and entered the trees.
