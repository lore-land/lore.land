Chapter Nine: Shards of Nine Honks

In which the ninth honk is missing, and warms.

An unspent wish is an unsounded note. Everything else in the set was arranged around its silence.

3 min read

Boof

ninth honk

1 min

Boonhonk is an old art with young shoes, and on the ridge above the town the Fool finally showed Boof its bones. He laid his set out on the slates shard by shard — a lineage in nine pieces — for the unit of the art is the honk: a trope sampled from the old tales, sounded in a new mouth, and returned changed, with interest. Each shard hummed the shape of the story it was cut from, and as he set them down in a crescent, Boof could hear where every chamber of their road had come from and where it had been sent back richer.

The law of the genre is short enough for a dog to carry: every trope you sample must be returned changed, with interest. A honk that only repeats is a debt; a honk that returns is a harvest.

The Fool laid out the shards. Three wishes; a trickster in a tree; a genie lately out of business. A vizier, baked bitter. Two claims splitting a floor. Glass streets, a blueberry oath, a tavern game that couldn't price its quietest player. He touched each piece as he named it, letting the little rings overlap. “Eight.” He slid them together. One space remained. Boof looked at the space, then down at her sling.

The egg fitted without a push. Eight shards answered together, their separate sounds drawing into one hum. Boof felt it through her nose. Here were the places she had walked, close enough to touch, with a space for what she still carried. The Fool let her look. For once, he had nothing to add. The slates still held the day's warmth. Below them, somebody was closing shutters, one clap after another down the street, while a late cart worked its way toward the gate. Boof could have followed any of those sounds to an ordinary evening. Instead she stayed with the pieces. This one caught the light like the grove; that edge brought back the hard feel of the market stone beneath her paw. They made a shape together, but she remembered the distance between them, the meals and mud and waiting. No one could have handed her that at the beginning.

"A honk is spent breath," the Fool said, lifting the egg free and settling it back in her sling. "A wish is breath still held. Same lineage, opposite ends." The ninth honk would sound when the wish was spent — no sooner, and by the Blueberry Oath, not before the want was true. She left the eight shards singing softly on the rooftop and turned down toward the northern pass, where the great timber spine of Bone.land waited to be asked what a small thing can hold.

Down the ridge, Boof carried the unsounded note against her ribs. The others could stay on the roof and sing to the chimneys. This one needed walking. She had promised to wait until the want was true; now she knew the silence had been waiting with her.

She did not need the eight shards to know her place in the lineage. She had the ninth, folded in its shell against her ribs, and it was leading straight into the massive timber spine of Bone.land, where nothing is trusted until it has proved it can bear weight.
