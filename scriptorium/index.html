One line

Your way through

Set a chapter aside, or keep two people out of the same room, and the book goes round them. The chapter after one you stepped over says what it held. The lanes keep to the same line. It is all written as one line of Spw, so you can read it, change it by hand, or pass it to someone for a visit. Nothing you have kept is touched, and every choice can be taken back.

This needs scripts. Without them the book reads whole, in order.

The book's Spw, projected · arcs and loops

The projection

Everything in the book is written in Spw, and everything written there points somewhere else: a chapter to its rooms, a room to the wonder first met in it, the wonder to the rooms it comes back to, a line the pies let slip to the room it is about. Here it is laid on a disk that is larger inside than it looks. What you stand on sits at the centre; its neighbours ring it; theirs crowd toward the rim and never reach it, so there is always more beyond. Tap anything to walk there, along an arc. Some walks come back to where they began.

At first a thing shows only its spell. Stand on it and it says what it means. Walk a loop through it and it tells you what it is for. Arrow keys move among the neighbours, Enter walks there, and L lists the loops.

The projection needs scripts to draw. Without them, the same web is walkable in the almanac and at the end of every chapter, from Chapter One on.

How a chamber is made

A mystery is produced, not announced. Harvest a pressure, measure it so the count can be checked, file a key that only gets smaller, then emit a turn that leaves a slip. A false slip is refused. That refusal is the proof the walk means it.

Harvest falls out as a scene. A pressure becomes a scene the land can hold.

How a chamber is seeded

  1. Name a pressure. A real process or value under stress—audit, delivery, negotiation, memory.
  2. Dress it in the land. Map-house, long table, parcel gate, grove: names a reader can walk into.
  3. Leave a second reading folded. Recognition for those who have lived the pressure; pure adventure for everyone else.
  4. Set the stone on the calendar. Releases on the 13th and 26th keep the monument on a rhythm the audience can trust.

How a chapter carries wonder

The wonder path

Every chapter answers the next question a reader asks, in five movements, each in its turn. And each one holds a question one edit away from something it already says: a line you can guess about now and check later, or keep open on purpose. The answer is yours to give.

  1. Promise · What is said? A seal, claim, or invitation sets an expectation people can repeat.
  2. Encounter · Where is it felt? A chamber, a room, or a threshold lets the promise meet a real need.
  3. Evidence · What makes it believable? Consequences, records, behavior, and material choices show whether the claim survives contact.
  4. Memory · What travels afterward? A motif, phrase, feeling, or useful result gives people something distinct to carry.
  5. Return · Why come back? Rhythm, unfinished responsibility, and renewed value create continuity without demanding constant attention. Nothing here counts your days. Wonder sets the pace: you stop when there is something to look at.

User stories, as spells

The loom of stories

Everyone this book is for, written as one Spw spell each: who they are, what they ask, what might be the case, what they do, what they tune, what is measured, what it becomes, who is with them, where they stand, what it is made of, and what they value. Laid side by side, the stories rhyme along some dimensions and not others. Touch a value to see who shares it and the pattern they make, written back as a spell; press ▶ on any spell to hear its shape. The stories live in stories.spw.

Reading the stories…

Marks stay marked

Nothing in the book is paid for today. If anything ever is, it will wear a mark beside the story saying who, for what and since when: a mark buys a place, never a word.

How a mark is kept
  • A mark is legible as patronage, never disguised as narration.
  • A mark keeps its provenance: who, for what, and since when.
  • A mark buys a place beside the story, never a word in it; editorial judgment stays with the scriptorium.
  • A mark’s link is a marked threshold, never a hidden door.
  • A mark never reaches the story’s words, the audience gate, or a reader’s own marks.

The marks are kept in marks.json: each build sets every mark beside the pages it names, and the book’s check holds it to these lines.
