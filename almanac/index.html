What you have found in Boon.land

Every chapter names things worth keeping: creatures of the wild places, old things the land keeps, things a scene leaves behind, contrivances of the built places, bracework — the makers' habit of writing what they mean to make in sigils and braces — and the sayings they live by. The first time a chapter names one, it is marked with a dotted line. Tap it, and it comes here. Most of them live in more than one place; finding one again somewhere new is how the almanac fills in.

@ Bestiary

The wild places keep creatures, and every one of them watches back a little.

    . Antiques

    Old made things the land keeps: who made each one, who keeps it now, and what it remembers. Some were made in one chapter and are kept in another.

      * Artifacts

      What a scene leaves behind, marked by what happened in it, and what it asks of whoever picks it up. Some are found in the chapters; some are made in the lanes, by what a carry does.

        $ Contrivances

        Where people build, they build things that help a little and cost a little, and use them still: needles, ladders, rules, dividers, boxes.

          & Bracework

          Makers write what they mean to build before they build it, so it can be leaned on, read by strangers, or combined into something nobody planned. Braces say what is held together; a sigil says how.

            # Sayings

            What the land's makers say while they work: rules about ink and glass, chalk and oak, mornings and roads. Each one travels from place to place, and each has a second reading, in the workshop where this book is made.

              = Palettes

              Every chapter has its own colours and lettering, mixed from a laminated folio (or from its plate, where no folio fits yet). Finish a chapter and you keep a layer of its palette, one for each way of finishing it: read to its end for the colour, carry the pie through it for the type, keep its own wonders for the light. The chapter wears what you have kept. Each palette can be copied, for a painter or for a web page.

                @ Cards

                Everyone and everything in the book has a card. A card turns the first time its name comes into view in a chapter, or the first time you meet it in the lanes of Exactly One Pie; both keep the same deck.

                  The combining bench

                  a night-school game

                  Choose a container and a sigil, then two things. Close the braces and the box fogs into a small realm. A combine holds only what both things agree on, and it runs on borrowed warmth. The things you have found in the chapters join the hat of words; a few common words are always in it.
